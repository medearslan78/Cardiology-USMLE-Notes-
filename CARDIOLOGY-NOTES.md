# 1. ISCHEMIC HEART DISEASE / ACS

## 🔑 Key Concepts

### Definition
Reduced blood supply to myocardium due to coronary artery disease (CAD), usually from atherosclerosis.

### ACS Spectrum
| Type | Troponin | ECG | Description |
|------|----------|-----|-------------|
| **Unstable Angina** | Normal | ST depression / Normal | Plaque rupture, no necrosis |
| **NSTEMI** | ↑ Elevated | ST depression / T inversion | Partial occlusion, subendocardial |
| **STEMI** | ↑↑ High | ST elevation | Complete occlusion, transmural |

### ECG Changes Timeline in STEMI
| Time | ECG Finding |
|------|-------------|
| **Minutes (Hyperacute)** | Tall peaked T waves |
| **Hours (1-24h)** | ST elevation, Q waves begin |
| **Days (24-72h)** | T wave inversion |
| **Weeks** | Pathological Q waves persist |

### Coronary Artery Territories
| Artery | Area Supplied | ECG Leads |
|--------|--------------|-----------|
| **LAD** | Anterior wall, septum | V1-V4 |
| **RCA** | Inferior wall, SA/AV node | II, III, aVF |
| **LCx** | Lateral wall | I, aVL, V5-V6 |

### Cardiac Biomarkers
| Marker | Rises | Peaks | Returns to Normal |
|--------|-------|-------|-------------------|
| **Troponin I/T** | 3-6 hrs | 24 hrs | 7-10 days |
| **CK-MB** | 3-6 hrs | 24 hrs | 48-72 hrs |
| **Myoglobin** | 1-2 hrs | 6-8 hrs | 24 hrs |

> 🔴 **High Yield:** Troponin = most sensitive AND specific for MI. CK-MB used to detect reinfarction.

### Management of STEMI
```
MONA + Reperfusion
M — Morphine (pain)
O — Oxygen (if SpO2 <90%)
N — Nitrates (sublingual)
A — Aspirin 325mg stat

REPERFUSION:
• Primary PCI (preferred if available within 90 min)
• Thrombolytics if PCI not available within 120 min

ANTIPLATELET: Aspirin + Clopidogrel/Ticagrelor
ANTICOAGULANT: Heparin/LMWH
BETA BLOCKER: Within 24 hours
ACE INHIBITOR: Within 24 hours
STATIN: High dose (Atorvastatin 80mg)
```

### Complications of MI
| Time | Complication |
|------|-------------|
| **24 hours** | Arrhythmias (VF most common cause of death) |
| **1-3 days** | Fibrinous pericarditis |
| **3-5 days** | Papillary muscle rupture → MR |
| **3-7 days** | Free wall rupture → tamponade |
| **5-10 days** | Ventricular septal rupture |
| **Weeks** | Dressler syndrome (weeks-months) |

## ❓ NBME-STYLE MCQs — ACS

**Q1.** A 58-year-old man presents with crushing chest pain radiating to the left arm for 2 hours. ECG shows ST elevation in leads II, III, aVF. Which coronary artery is most likely occluded?

- A) Left anterior descending artery
- B) Left circumflex artery
- C) **Right coronary artery ✅**
- D) Left main coronary artery

> **Explanation:** Inferior STEMI (II, III, aVF) = RCA territory. RCA also supplies SA and AV nodes.

---

**Q2.** A patient presents 6 hours after chest pain. Troponin is elevated. ECG shows ST depression in V1-V4. What is the diagnosis?

- A) STEMI
- B) **NSTEMI ✅**
- C) Unstable Angina
- D) Pericarditis

> **Explanation:** Elevated troponin + ST depression (not elevation) = NSTEMI. ST elevation = STEMI.

---

**Q3.** Which cardiac biomarker is BEST to detect reinfarction 3 days after an initial MI?

- A) Troponin I
- B) Myoglobin
- C) **CK-MB ✅**
- D) LDH

> **Explanation:** CK-MB returns to normal in 48-72 hrs. A new rise indicates reinfarction. Troponin stays elevated for days.

---

**Q4.** A 65-year-old develops a new holosystolic murmur 4 days after MI. Echo shows mitral regurgitation. What is the most likely cause?

- A) Aortic dissection
- B) Ventricular septal defect
- C) **Papillary muscle rupture ✅**
- D) Dressler syndrome

> **Explanation:** Papillary muscle rupture → acute MR, occurs 3-5 days post-MI. Posteromedial papillary muscle most vulnerable (single blood supply from RCA).

---

**Q5.** A 70-year-old man presents 6 weeks after MI with fever, pleuritic chest pain, and pericardial friction rub. What is the diagnosis?

- A) Reinfarction
- B) Pulmonary embolism
- C) **Dressler Syndrome ✅**
- D) Aortic stenosis

> **Explanation:** Dressler syndrome = autoimmune pericarditis weeks after MI. Treat with NSAIDs/Aspirin.

---

**Q6.** Most common cause of death within the FIRST HOUR of acute MI?

- A) Cardiogenic shock
- B) Free wall rupture
- C) **Ventricular fibrillation ✅**
- D) Cardiac tamponade

> **Explanation:** VF is the most common cause of early death in MI — treated with defibrillation.

# 2. HEART FAILURE (CCF)

## 🔑 Key Concepts

### Definition
Heart's inability to pump sufficient blood to meet body's metabolic demands.

### Types
| Feature | Systolic (HFrEF) | Diastolic (HFpEF) |
|---------|-----------------|------------------|
| **EF** | <40% | ≥50% |
| **Problem** | Reduced contractility | Impaired relaxation |
| **Causes** | MI, DCM, valve disease | HTN, HCM, restrictive CM |
| **Heart size** | Dilated | Normal or small |

### Symptoms
**Left Heart Failure (Backward):**
- Dyspnea on exertion
- Orthopnea
- Paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnea (PND)
- Pulmonary edema / crackles

**Right Heart Failure (Backward):**
- JVD (Jugular vein distension)
- Peripheral edema (pitting)
- Hepatomegaly / ascites
- Anorexia

### NYHA Classification
| Class | Symptoms |
|-------|----------|
| **I** | No symptoms with ordinary activity |
| **II** | Mild symptoms with ordinary activity |
| **III** | Marked symptoms with less-than-ordinary activity |
| **IV** | Symptoms at rest |

### Key Investigations
- **BNP/NT-proBNP** — Best initial test; elevated in HF
- **Echo** — Gold standard; shows EF
- **CXR** — Cardiomegaly, pulmonary congestion, Kerley B lines

### Management of HFrEF (EF <40%)
```
CORE THERAPY (reduce mortality):
1. ACE Inhibitor / ARB (if ACE intolerant)
   → ARNI (Sacubitril/Valsartan) if still symptomatic
2. Beta Blocker (Carvedilol, Metoprolol, Bisoprolol)
3. Aldosterone Antagonist (Spironolactone)
4. SGLT2 Inhibitor (Dapagliflozin, Empagliflozin)

SYMPTOM RELIEF:
• Diuretics (Furosemide) — for fluid overload
• Digoxin — reduces hospitalizations

DEVICE THERAPY:
• ICD if EF <35% (prevent sudden death)
• CRT if EF <35% + LBBB (resynchronization)
```

### High-Yield Drug Points
| Drug | Key Point |
|------|-----------|
| **ACE Inhibitors** | First line; reduce mortality; cause cough |
| **ARBs** | Use if ACE intolerant (no cough) |
| **Carvedilol** | Non-selective BB; best evidence in HF |
| **Spironolactone** | K-sparing diuretic; watch hyperkalemia |
| **Furosemide** | Loop diuretic; symptom relief |
| **Digoxin** | Positive inotrope; narrow therapeutic window |

## ❓ NBME-STYLE MCQs — HEART FAILURE

**Q1.** A 60-year-old man with EF of 30% is already on ACE inhibitor and beta blocker. What drug should be added next to reduce mortality?

- A) Furosemide
- B) Digoxin
- C) **Spironolactone ✅**
- D) Amlodipine

> **Explanation:** Aldosterone antagonist (spironolactone) reduces mortality in HFrEF when added to ACE-I + BB. Monitor potassium.

---

**Q2.** Which finding on CXR is MOST specific for left heart failure?

- A) Pleural effusion
- B) Cardiomegaly
- C) **Kerley B lines ✅**
- D) Hilar enlargement

> **Explanation:** Kerley B lines = interstitial pulmonary edema from elevated pulmonary capillary wedge pressure in LHF.

---

**Q3.** A patient with HF develops a dry cough on lisinopril. What is the BEST alternative?

- A) Atenolol
- B) Spironolactone
- C) **Losartan (ARB) ✅**
- D) Hydralazine

> **Explanation:** ACE inhibitor causes dry cough due to bradykinin accumulation. Switch to ARB (Losartan/Valsartan).

---

**Q4.** Best initial test to CONFIRM diagnosis of heart failure?

- A) ECG
- B) CXR
- C) **BNP level ✅**
- D) Cardiac catheterization

> **Explanation:** BNP/NT-proBNP is elevated in HF and best initial screening test. Echo is gold standard for confirmation.

---

**Q5.** A 55-year-old hypertensive woman has dyspnea but EF is 60% on echo. What type of heart failure?

- A) Systolic HF
- B) **Diastolic HF (HFpEF) ✅**
- C) Right heart failure
- D) High-output HF

> **Explanation:** HFpEF = preserved EF (≥50%) with impaired diastolic relaxation. Common in hypertension, elderly women.

# 3. HYPERTENSION

## 🔑 Key Concepts

### Classification (JNC 8 / ACC/AHA 2017)
| Category | Systolic | Diastolic |
|----------|---------|-----------|
| Normal | <120 | <80 |
| Elevated | 120-129 | <80 |
| Stage 1 HTN | 130-139 | 80-89 |
| Stage 2 HTN | ≥140 | ≥90 |
| Hypertensive Crisis | >180 | >120 |

### Hypertensive Emergency vs Urgency
| | Emergency | Urgency |
|--|-----------|---------|
| **BP** | >180/120 | >180/120 |
| **End-organ damage** | YES | No |
| **Treatment** | IV drugs (ICU) | Oral drugs |
| **Goal** | Reduce MAP by 25% in 1 hr | Reduce over 24-48 hrs |

### First-Line Antihypertensives
| Patient Type | Drug of Choice |
|-------------|---------------|
| **General** | Thiazide, CCB, ACE-I, ARB |
| **Black patients** | Thiazide or CCB (NOT ACE-I alone) |
| **Diabetes** | ACE-I or ARB |
| **CKD** | ACE-I or ARB |
| **Heart failure** | ACE-I + BB + Spironolactone |
| **Post-MI** | BB + ACE-I |
| **Pregnancy** | Methyldopa, Nifedipine, Labetalol |
| **BPH + HTN** | Alpha blocker (Prazosin) |

### Secondary Causes of HTN
| Cause | Clue |
|-------|------|
| **Renal artery stenosis** | Young woman (fibromuscular) or atherosclerosis; bruit |
| **Primary hyperaldosteronism** | Hypokalemia + HTN; ↑ aldosterone, ↓ renin |
| **Pheochromocytoma** | Episodic HTN, headache, sweating, palpitations |
| **Cushing syndrome** | Central obesity, striae, buffalo hump |
| **Coarctation of aorta** | Upper extremity HTN, rib notching |
| **Hypothyroidism** | Diastolic HTN |

## ❓ NBME-STYLE MCQs — HYPERTENSION

**Q1.** A 45-year-old Black man with BP 155/95 and no comorbidities. Best first-line drug?

- A) Lisinopril
- B) Metoprolol
- C) **Amlodipine (CCB) ✅**
- D) Losartan

> **Explanation:** Black patients respond poorly to ACE inhibitors/ARBs as monotherapy. CCBs or thiazides are preferred.

---

**Q2.** A 35-year-old woman has HTN with episodic headaches, palpitations, and diaphoresis. BP fluctuates. What is the diagnosis?

- A) Primary HTN
- B) Renal artery stenosis
- C) **Pheochromocytoma ✅**
- D) Coarctation of aorta

> **Explanation:** Classic triad of pheo = Headache + Hypertension + Hyperhidrosis (sweating). Diagnose with 24-hr urine catecholamines/metanephrines.

---

**Q3.** A pregnant woman at 32 weeks has BP 160/100. Which drug is CONTRAINDICATED?

- A) Methyldopa
- B) Nifedipine
- C) Labetalol
- D) **Lisinopril ✅**

> **Explanation:** ACE inhibitors are teratogenic in 2nd/3rd trimester — cause renal agenesis, oligohydramnios. Safe drugs: Methyldopa, Nifedipine, Labetalol.

---

**Q4.** A patient with HTN has hypokalemia despite no diuretics, and aldosterone is high with low renin. Diagnosis?

- A) Cushing syndrome
- B) Renovascular HTN
- C) **Primary hyperaldosteronism (Conn syndrome) ✅**
- D) Pheochromocytoma

> **Explanation:** High aldosterone + Low renin = Primary hyperaldosteronism. Hypokalemia due to aldosterone-driven K+ wasting.

# 4. VALVULAR HEART DISEASE

## 🔑 Key Concepts

### Murmur Quick Reference
| Valve Lesion | Type | Location | Radiation | Key Feature |
|-------------|------|----------|-----------|-------------|
| **Aortic Stenosis** | Systolic ejection | RUSB | Carotids | Pulsus parvus et tardus |
| **Aortic Regurgitation** | Diastolic decrescendo | LLSB | — | Wide pulse pressure |
| **Mitral Stenosis** | Mid-diastolic rumble | Apex | — | Opening snap |
| **Mitral Regurgitation** | Holosystolic | Apex | Axilla | S3 gallop |
| **MVP** | Late systolic | Apex | — | Mid-systolic click |
| **Tricuspid Regurg** | Holosystolic | LLSB | — | ↑ with inspiration |
| **VSD** | Holosystolic | LLSB | — | Harsh murmur |
| **ASD** | Systolic ejection | LUSB | — | Fixed split S2 |
| **PDA** | Continuous machinery | LUSB | — | Bounding pulses |

### Aortic Stenosis — High Yield
- **Cause:** Calcific (elderly), Bicuspid AV (young), Rheumatic
- **Symptoms triad:** Angina → Syncope → HF (SAD)
- **Survival after symptoms:** Angina 5yr, Syncope 3yr, HF 2yr
- **Management:** AVR (surgery) or TAVR (transcatheter)
- **Important:** Do NOT give nitrates/vasodilators (worsen)

### Mitral Stenosis — High Yield
- **Cause:** Almost always Rheumatic fever
- **Complications:** AFib, pulmonary HTN, systemic emboli
- **Opening snap:** High-pitched; shorter A2-OS interval = more severe
- **Treatment:** Balloon valvuloplasty (if no MR/calcium), MVR

- A) HCM
- B) Mitral regurgitation
- C) **Aortic stenosis ✅**
- D) Aortic regurgitation

> **Explanation:** Classic AS = SAD (Syncope, Angina, Dyspnea) + crescendo-decrescendo systolic murmur at RUSB → carotids.

---

- A) Loud S1
- B) Opening snap
- C) **Short A2-OS interval ✅**
- D) Atrial fibrillation

> **Explanation:** AR = wide pulse pressure (water hammer pulse), blowing decrescendo diastolic murmur at LLSB.

# 5. ARRHYTHMIAS

## 🔑 Key Concepts

### Approach to ECG
```
Rate → Rhythm → P waves → PR interval → QRS → ST/T waves
```

### Common Arrhythmias
| Arrhythmia | Rate | Rhythm | Key Feature | Treatment |
|-----------|------|--------|-------------|-----------|
| **AFib** | 350-600 atrial | Irregularly irregular | No P waves | Rate control: BB/CCB; Rhythm: Amiodarone; Anticoag |
| **AFlutter** | 250-350 atrial | Regular | Sawtooth P waves | Cardioversion |
| **SVT** | 150-250 | Regular | P buried in QRS | Vagal maneuvers → Adenosine |
| **VT** | 100-250 | Regular | Wide QRS | Amiodarone; if pulseless → Defibrillate |
| **VF** | Chaotic | Chaotic | No organized QRS | Immediate defibrillation |
| **Heart Block 1°** | Normal | Regular | PR >200ms | None |
| **Heart Block 2° Mobitz I** | Normal | Irregular | PR progressively ↑ then dropped beat | None |
| **Heart Block 2° Mobitz II** | Normal | Irregular | Sudden dropped beat without PR change | Pacemaker |
| **Complete Heart Block** | <60 | Regular | No relationship P-QRS | Pacemaker |

### AFib Management
```
RATE CONTROL (if stable, chronic):
• Beta blockers (Metoprolol)
• Non-DHP CCBs (Diltiazem, Verapamil)
• Digoxin (in HF)

RHYTHM CONTROL:
• Amiodarone, Flecainide, Sotalol

ANTICOAGULATION:
• CHA₂DS₂-VASc score ≥2 → Anticoagulate
• DOACs (Apixaban, Rivaroxaban) preferred over Warfarin

CARDIOVERSION:
• If AFib <48 hrs → Can cardiovert immediately
• If AFib >48 hrs → Anticoagulate for 3 weeks first (or TEE to exclude clot)
```

# 6. PERICARDIAL DISEASE

## 🔑 Key Concepts

### Pericarditis vs Cardiac Tamponade vs Constrictive Pericarditis
| Feature | Pericarditis | Tamponade | Constrictive |
|---------|-------------|-----------|-------------|
| **Cause** | Viral (Coxsackie), TB, Uremia | Malignancy, Trauma, Uremia | TB (most common worldwide) |
| **Pain** | Pleuritic, relieved leaning forward | None (shock picture) | None |
| **ECG** | Diffuse saddle-shaped ST elevation | Low voltage + electrical alternans | Normal |
| **JVD** | Absent | Present | Present |
| **Kussmaul sign** | Absent | Absent | Present |
| **Pulsus paradoxus** | Absent | Present (>10mmHg) | Absent/mild |
| **Treatment** | NSAIDs + Colchicine | Pericardiocentesis | Pericardiectomy |

### Beck's Triad (Tamponade)
> **Hypotension + JVD + Muffled heart sounds**

- A) STEMI
- B) **Pericarditis ✅**

> **Explanation:** Pericarditis = pleuritic pain, relieved leaning forward, diffuse saddle-shaped ST elevation (not localized like STEMI), PR depression.

# 7. CARDIOMYOPATHIES

## 🔑 Key Concepts

| Feature | Dilated (DCM) | Hypertrophic (HCM) | Restrictive |
|---------|--------------|-------------------|-------------|
| **EF** | ↓ | Normal or ↑ | Normal |
| **Ventricle** | Dilated, thin wall | Hypertrophied, especially septum | Normal size, stiff |
| **Cause** | Viral, alcohol, postpartum | Genetic (sarcomere mutations) | Amyloid, sarcoid, hemochromatosis |
| **Key feature** | S3 gallop, MR | Dynamic outflow obstruction | Kussmaul sign |
| **Treatment** | HF treatment | BB or Verapamil; avoid vasodilators | Treat underlying cause |

### HCM — High Yield
- **Murmur:** Systolic ejection murmur at LLSB
- **Increases with:** Valsalva, standing (↓ preload → worse obstruction)
- **Decreases with:** Squatting, lying down (↑ preload → less obstruction)
- **Most common cause of sudden cardiac death in young athletes**
- **Treatment:** BB (first line), Verapamil; avoid digoxin, nitrates

---

## ❓ NBME-STYLE MCQs — CARDIOMYOPATHY

**Q1.** A 22-year-old basketball player collapses during a game and dies. Autopsy shows asymmetric septal hypertrophy. Diagnosis?

- A) Dilated cardiomyopathy
- B) Aortic stenosis
- C) **Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy ✅**
- D) Myocarditis

> **Explanation:** HCM = most common cause of sudden cardiac death in young athletes. Asymmetric septal hypertrophy is hallmark.

---

**Q2.** A patient with HCM murmur increases in intensity with which maneuver?

- A) Squatting
- B) Raising legs passively
- C) **Valsalva maneuver ✅**
- D) Hand grip

> **Explanation:** Valsalva ↓ preload → ↑ HCM obstruction → louder murmur. (Opposite for most other murmurs.)

# 8. CONGENITAL HEART DISEASE

## 🔑 Key Concepts

### Cyanotic vs Acyanotic
| Type | Examples | Shunt |
|------|---------|-------|
| **Acyanotic** | VSD, ASD, PDA, Coarctation | Left → Right |
| **Cyanotic** | Tetralogy of Fallot, TGA, Truncus arteriosus | Right → Left |

### Most Common Congenital Heart Defects
| Defect | Key Feature | Treatment |
|--------|------------|-----------|
| **VSD** | Most common CHD; holosystolic murmur LLSB; may close spontaneously | Surgical repair if large |
| **ASD** | Fixed split S2; RV volume overload | Closure |
| **PDA** | Continuous machinery murmur; ↑ by prematurity | Indomethacin (closes PDA); Prostaglandin E1 (keeps open) |
| **Tetralogy of Fallot** | Cyanosis, RVH, boot-shaped heart; Tet spells; squatting relieves | Surgical repair |
| **TGA** | Cyanosis at birth; egg-on-string heart; needs PDA to survive | Prostaglandin E1 then arterial switch |
| **Coarctation of Aorta** | HTN in arms, weak femoral pulse, rib notching | Surgery/stenting |

### Tetralogy of Fallot — 4 Components
```
P — Pulmonary stenosis (most important)
R — Right ventricular hypertrophy
O — Overriding aorta
V — VSD
```

## ❓ NBME-STYLE MCQs — CONGENITAL

- B) **Indomethacin ✅**
- C) Digoxin

> **Explanation:** PDA = continuous machinery murmur. Indomethacin (NSAID, inhibits prostaglandins) closes PDA. PGE1 keeps it open.

- A) TGA

> **Explanation:** TOF = boot-shaped heart, RVH, squatting relieves cyanosis (increases SVR → more blood to lungs).

# 9. SHOCK

## 🔑 Key Concepts

### Types of Shock
| Type | CO | PCWP | SVR | Example |
|------|----|----|-----|---------|
| **Hypovolemic** | ↓ | ↓ | ↑ | Hemorrhage, dehydration |
| **Cardiogenic** | ↓ | ↑ | ↑ | MI, severe HF |
| **Distributive (Septic)** | ↑ | ↓ | ↓ | Infection |
| **Obstructive** | ↓ | ↑ | ↑ | PE, tamponade, tension pneumo |
| **Neurogenic** | ↓ | ↓ | ↓ | Spinal cord injury |

### Management
| Shock Type | First-Line Treatment |
|-----------|---------------------|
| **Hypovolemic** | IV fluids / Blood transfusion |
| **Cardiogenic** | Inotropes (Dobutamine); Vasopressors |
| **Septic** | IV fluids → Norepinephrine → Antibiotics |
| **Anaphylactic** | Epinephrine IM 0.5mg |
| **Cardiac Tamponade** | Pericardiocentesis |

---

## ❓ NBME-STYLE MCQs — SHOCK

**Q1.** A patient post-MI develops hypotension with elevated PCWP and low CO. What is the diagnosis?

- A) Septic shock
- B) Hypovolemic shock
- C) **Cardiogenic shock ✅**
- D) Distributive shock

> **Explanation:** Cardiogenic shock = ↓CO + ↑PCWP (backed-up blood) + ↑SVR. Treat with Dobutamine.

---

**Q2.** A patient with bee sting develops hypotension, urticaria, and bronchospasm. Immediate treatment?

- A) IV corticosteroids
- B) Diphenhydramine
- C) **Epinephrine IM ✅**
- D) IV fluids

> **Explanation:** Anaphylaxis → Epinephrine 0.5mg IM (thigh) is the FIRST and most important treatment.

# 10. LIPID DISORDERS & ATHEROSCLEROSIS

## 🔑 Key Concepts

### Lipid Targets
| Patient | LDL Goal |
|---------|---------|
| Very high risk (ASCVD, DM + RF) | <70 mg/dL |
| High risk (DM, 10-yr risk >10%) | <100 mg/dL |
| Moderate risk | <130 mg/dL |

### Statins — High Yield
- **Mechanism:** Inhibit HMG-CoA reductase
- **High intensity:** Atorvastatin 40-80mg, Rosuvastatin 20-40mg
- **Side effects:** Myopathy, rhabdomyolysis, ↑LFTs
- **Contraindicated in:** Pregnancy

### When to Start Statin (ACC/AHA)
1. Clinical ASCVD
2. LDL ≥190 mg/dL
3. Diabetes age 40-75 with LDL 70-189
4. 10-year ASCVD risk ≥7.5% (age 40-75)
